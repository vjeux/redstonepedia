Enchanting table calculator

The enchanting table is not random. What it offers comes from a hidden number of yours, the enchanting seed, which changes only when you enchant. Read the table a few times and the calculator finds that number; then it tells you how many items to drop so that your next enchant gives exactly what you picked.

Before you start

Enchant any cheap item once (a book in the top slot costs 1 level and 1 lapis). From then until you are done, do not sprint, take damage, eat or drink, swim, or leave the world, and keep parrots off your shoulder: those use the same hidden numbers (so does experience mending a damaged item). Walking, jumping, opening the table and moving items are fine.

1. Find your enchanting seed

Put an item you can enchant in the table and write down the level each of the three slots asks for. Then change how many bookshelves the table sees (a torch or a carpet between a shelf and the table hides it) and write them down again. Low bookshelf counts tell the most; with 15 the bottom slot always asks for 30. It usually takes about seven readings; with the hint you see when you point at a slot, three or four.

2. Enchant something cheap, then find the next seed

Enchant any cheap item in the top slot, then read the table again the same way. Only 65,536 seeds can come next, so three or four readings are usually enough (one or two with the hints).

3. Pick the enchantments

For each enchantment: × it must not have it, – either way, or the lowest level you want.

How it works

The table seeds its own random numbers with your enchanting seed, so the same seed, item and bookshelf count always show the same offers. The seed changes only when you enchant: the game then draws the next number from your player's own random generator. Two seeds in a row give that generator away, so every later seed is known. Each press of Q draws four numbers from the same generator, so the number of items you drop picks which seed your next enchant draws: drop them, enchant anything cheap (that draws the seed the calculator picked), set the bookshelves, and enchant.

Checked against the real 26.3 game: every offer of the table for 200 seeds, every enchantable item and every bookshelf count, and the whole method, item in hand, from reading the table to the final enchant. The trick was first found by Earthcomputer.
