Details: how it works, measurements and properties

In depth

Any input pulse, even a 1-tick one, gives an output pulse of at least L = 10 ticks (0.5 s), starting 1 tick (0.05 s) after the input turns on. It handles every input pulse of 2 ticks (0.1 s) (the shortest a real input makes) or longer.

Use it to make a brief signal (an observer's 2-tick pulse) last long enough for a slower part.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the demo on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same sequence is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2-tick pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the view adds it to show the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

Stretches any input, even the shortest input (2 ticks), into exactly 10 ticks (half a second), and footsteps or dropped items nearby never set it off: the input makes a note block play, a calibrated sculk sensor (a sound sensor) hears it and switches on for exactly 10 ticks however short or long the input was, and a comparator reading a chest tunes the sensor to that one kind of sound. To build it: fill the chest with 1111 items that stack to 64 (17 full stacks and 23 more), so the comparator gives 10 (the number shown over the chest in the 3D view). Good to know: the note block sounds on every input, and that sound is a vibration that sets off other sculk sensors within 16 blocks; the sensor still hears other machines' sounds of the same kind within 16 blocks (another note block, a door or trapdoor opening, a piston pushing, a lever or button switched on); "vibration-proof" means proof against players walking, jumping, dropping, placing and breaking blocks around it. Detail: the note block plays only when it becomes powered (note_block_play, frequency 10); a calibrated sensor with a back signal accepts that frequency only (the lever's block_deactivate is 9, footsteps 1, items landing 2, blocks broken or placed 12 and 13).
