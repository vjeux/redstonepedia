Details: how it works, measurements and properties

In depth

When the input turns off, the output gives one short pulse of L = 14 ticks (0.7 s), starting 6 ticks (0.3 s) after the input turned off. When the input turns on, nothing happens. It handles every input pulse of 6 ticks (0.3 s) or longer.

Use it to react to something stopping, e.g. a lever being switched back.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the demo on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same sequence is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2-tick pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the orange glass marker next to it glows while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Inputs and output over time

The input follows this schedule (the 3D view's demo plays the same). Lime: an input is on; orange: the output is on (taller = a stronger signal, 1 to 15). Time runs left to right in game ticks (20 ticks = 1 s).

Swipe the diagram sideways: the whole sequence lasts 913 ticks (45.65 s).
