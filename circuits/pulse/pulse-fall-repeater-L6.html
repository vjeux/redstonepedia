Try it

Hold IN under the view for a long press, tap it for a short one: the box marked OUT turns red while the output is on. Press play under the view to watch a demo on a loop; touch any other control to take over.

How this design does it: One pulse every time the input turns off, 4 blocks: a piston pulls an observer back to a repeater when the input turns off, where it fires, and the repeater stretches the pulse; pushed away when the input turns on, it fires into nothing.

Details: how it works, measurements and properties

In depth

When the input turns off, the output gives one short pulse of L = 6 ticks (0.3 s), starting 10 ticks (0.5 s) after the input turned off. When the input turns on, nothing happens. It handles every input pulse of 6 ticks (0.3 s) or longer.

Use it to react to something stopping, e.g. a lever being switched back.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the demo on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same sequence is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2-tick pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the view adds it to show the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Inputs and output over time

The input follows this schedule (the 3D view's demo plays the same). Lime: an input is on; orange: the output is on (taller = a stronger signal, 1 to 15). Time runs left to right in game ticks (20 ticks = 1 s).

Swipe the diagram sideways: the whole sequence lasts 635 ticks (31.75 s).
