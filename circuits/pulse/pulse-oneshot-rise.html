Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

When the input turns on, the output gives one short pulse of L = 18 gt (0.9 s), starting 6 gt (0.3 s) after the input turned on. When the input turns off, nothing happens. It handles every input pulse of 4 gt (0.2 s) or longer.

Use it to turn a lever (which stays on) into a single short pulse, e.g. to fire a piston once.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the orange glass marker next to it glows while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

One pulse of 18, 34, 50... ticks every time the input turns on: a piston-moved observer lights a copper bulb and a loop of repeaters switches it off; each extra row adds 16 ticks. Detail: extendable family oneshot-rise (n = 0): rising-edge with L = 18 + 16n gt, 17 + 4n blocks. a sticky piston pushes an observer whose back then faces a copper bulb; the observer fires on landing and lights the bulb; a comparator reads the bulb into a U of delay-4 repeaters that comes back into the bulb and turns it off one loop later (a bulb toggles only on rising edges of its power, so the loop's own falling edge does nothing); on the falling edge the observer is pulled back and fires into nothing. Extension: lengthen the repeater U by one row: one more delay-4 repeater (on stone) in each column. Generator: mctas circuit pulse family oneshot-rise --n N --out FILE.
