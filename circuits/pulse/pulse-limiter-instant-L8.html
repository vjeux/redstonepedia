Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

However long the input stays on, the output pulse lasts at most L = 8 gt (0.4 s); it starts 0 gt (0 s) after the input turns on. (An input shorter than L may come out shorter.) It handles every input pulse of 2 gt (the shortest a real input makes) or longer.

Use it to cut a long signal down to a short, fixed pulse.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

metricvaluewhat it means
L8 gt (0.4 s)the length of the output pulse

min-pulse2 gt (the shortest a real input makes)the shortest input pulse handled correctly (every longer one works too)

Notes from its designer

The fastest pulse limiter: any input reaches the output in the very same tick (delay 0) and is cut to 8 ticks if it is longer, 8 blocks. The input lights the output at once through two dusts and a stone beside the output; a repeater passes the input to a sticky piston 8 ticks later, which pushes that stone away and cuts the output; when the input turns off, the piston pulls the stone back. Detail: a pushed block conducts nothing from the moment it moves; the repeater's delay sets the length.
