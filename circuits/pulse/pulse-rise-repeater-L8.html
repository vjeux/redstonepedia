How this design does it: One pulse every time the input turns on, 4 blocks: a piston pushes an observer, which fires where it lands, and a repeater behind it stretches the pulse; when the input turns off, the observer is pulled back and fires into nothing.

Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

When the input turns on, the output gives one short pulse of L = 8 gt (0.4 s), starting 12 gt (0.6 s) after the input turned on. When the input turns off, nothing happens. It handles every input pulse of 4 gt (0.2 s) or longer.

Use it to turn a lever (which stays on) into a single short pulse, e.g. to fire a piston once.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

metricvaluewhat it means
L8 gt (0.4 s)the length of the output pulse

min-pulse4 gt (0.2 s)the shortest input pulse handled correctly (every longer one works too)

Notes from its designer

One pulse every time the input turns on, 4 blocks: a piston pushes an observer, which fires where it lands, and a repeater behind it stretches the pulse; when the input turns off, the observer is pulled back and fires into nothing. Detail: a sticky piston pushes an observer (facing sideways) whose back then faces a repeater: the observer fires 2 gt after it lands and the delay-4 repeater stretches its pulse to 8 gt; on the falling edge the observer is pulled back and fires into the empty cell beside the repeater.
