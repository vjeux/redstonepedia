How this design does it: Stretches any input of 4 ticks or more into one pulse of exactly 8 ticks per item in the first hopper (8 ticks to 24 seconds), always 13 blocks: a copper bulb remembers the input (a piston pushes an observer beside it, which fires once each time the input turns on); while the bulb is lit, its comparator locks the second hopper and unlocks the first, so the items pass one by one; when the first hopper is empty, its comparator goes off and a torch toggles the bulb off; then the items flow back.

Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

Any input pulse, even a 1-tick one, gives an output pulse of at least L = 128 gt (6.4 s), starting 6 gt (0.3 s) after the input turns on. It handles every input pulse of 4 gt (0.2 s) or longer.

Use it to make a brief signal (an observer's 2-tick pulse) last long enough for a slower part.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the orange glass marker next to it glows while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

Stretches any input of 4 ticks or more into one pulse of exactly 8 ticks per item in the first hopper (8 ticks to 24 seconds), always 13 blocks: a copper bulb remembers the input (a piston pushes an observer beside it, which fires once each time the input turns on); while the bulb is lit, its comparator locks the second hopper and unlocks the first, so the items pass one by one; when the first hopper is empty, its comparator goes off and a torch toggles the bulb off; then the items flow back. This one holds 16 items: 128 ticks. Detail: the bulb's comparator powers the stone above the second hopper (locking it) and turns off the torch on that stone, which locked the first hopper; the first hopper's comparator powers the block whose torch lights the bulb from below when that hopper empties.
