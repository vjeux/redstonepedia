Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

It unpacks shulker boxes. The judge puts a chest of full shulker boxes at the design's BOXES cell; the machine places a box, empties it into the chest at its ITEMS cell, and sends the empty box to the chest at its OUTPUT cell. In the test the judge supplies three full boxes of 64-stack items (1728 cobblestone a box) and every item and all three empty boxes must arrive, nothing lost or duplicated, at 4 positions and 4 rotations in the real game.

It moves 2.50 items per second: one box every 13838 gt (11 min 31.9 s); the first box arrives 13859 gt (11 min 32.95 s) after the start.

It also passes these scenarios (each a separate run of the judge):

  • always-on: no lever: it runs by itself
  • 16-stack: 16-stack items too (snowballs, ender pearls, eggs: 432 a box)
  • unstackable: unstackable items too (tools, armour: 27 a box)
  • colours: boxes keep their colour and their name
  • box-starved: nothing is lost while the empty-box supply runs out; it resumes when boxes come back
  • drop-free: box items never lie on the ground
  • tileable(1):

How to try it

The machine runs in the view. The view runs it the way the judge does, without end: the chest at the cell tagged BOXES is kept full of full shulker boxes, and the chests at ITEMS and OUT are emptied as the items and the empty boxes arrive. The items are unstackable (27 fill a box). In the view the first box goes out 251 gt (12.55 s) after the machine starts, then one every 230 gt (11.5 s). The numbers over each chest, hopper and dispenser show what it holds. Speed it up with the bar's speed button (up to 100×).

It starts by itself (no lever to flip). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom.

Notes from its designer

A one-block-wide shulker unloader you can line up side by side with no gap (22 blocks): a dispenser drops each full box onto a hopper that empties it, and when the box is empty a piston pushes a block into it, which breaks it into a second hopper that sends it on. How it works: as the 24-block unloader (a comparator reads the emptying hopper, a copper bulb remembers whether the box still has items, a torch fires the piston when it is empty, an observer on the piston fires the dispenser after each break, a comparator reading the dispenser starts it), with the search's savings: the pushed block is an observer that fires the dispenser itself, a lamp and a bulb stand in as the solid blocks, a piston and an observer serve as supports.
