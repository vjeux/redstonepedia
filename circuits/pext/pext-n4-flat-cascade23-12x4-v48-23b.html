Piston extender

reach 4 · 22 blocks

Turn the input on and a block is pushed 4 blocks out; turn it off and the block comes back exactly where it was. It goes out in 38 ticks (1.9 s) and comes back in 38 ticks (1.9 s).

Do not flip its lever back too soon: turned off within 20 ticks (1 s) of being turned on, it leaves blocks stranded. Wait until the block is all the way out.

Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

Turning the input on pushes a block 4 cells out of the machine; turning it off brings the block back exactly where it was, with nothing left behind. A plain piston only reaches 1.

Extending takes 38 gt (1.9 s), retracting 38 gt (1.9 s).

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: click the lever tagged IN (or the IN button of the bar) to switch it on or off.

Our test in the real game

The judge drives the input with this schedule and checks the output after every change; here it is simulated (the 3D view's demo plays the same). Lime: an input is on; orange: the output is on (taller = a stronger signal, 1 to 15). Blue: where the pushed block is, 0 = home, up to N = fully out (the dashed line); it must reach N while the input is on and come all the way back. Time runs left to right in game ticks (20 gt = 1 s).

Swipe the diagram sideways: the whole test lasts 2426 ticks (2 min 1.3 s).
