Try it

Flip the lever marked IN (or tap IN under the view): turn it on and watch the block go 2 blocks out; turn it off and it comes back. Press play under the view to watch our test on a loop; touch any other control to take over.

How this design does it: Reach 2 in 8 blocks, one block thick: a sticky piston pushes a slime frame carrying a second sticky piston, the block and an observer, and each time the frame lands the observer pulses the second piston for 2 ticks; going out, that pulse makes it push the block one more cell and leave it there; coming back, the frame drags the block back one cell (a slime block sits under it) and the pulse pulls it the rest of the way home.

Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

Turning the input on pushes a block 2 cells out of the machine; turning it off brings the block back exactly where it was, with nothing left behind. A plain piston only reaches 1.

Extending takes 9 gt (0.45 s), retracting 9 gt (0.45 s).

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

Our test in the real game

The judge drives the input with this schedule and checks the output after every change; here it is simulated (the 3D view's demo plays the same). Lime: an input is on; orange: the output is on (taller = a stronger signal, 1 to 15). Blue: where the pushed block is, 0 = home, up to N = fully out (the dashed line); it must reach N while the input is on and come all the way back. Time runs left to right in game ticks (20 gt = 1 s).

Swipe the diagram sideways: the whole test lasts 962 ticks (48.1 s).
