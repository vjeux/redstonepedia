Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

Turning the input on pushes a block 3 cells out of the machine; turning it off brings the block back exactly where it was, with nothing left behind. A plain piston only reaches 1.

Extending takes 55 gt (2.75 s), retracting 55 gt (2.75 s).

Notes from its designer

A 3-piston extender you can flip back at any time (50 blocks, 9x5x6): the block goes out 3 cells in 55 gt and back in 55 gt, and turning the lever off or on again early never strands a block. How it works: it grew out of the slime-free stack of pext-n3-sf-v1, three sticky pistons in a row: going out each one pushes the ones in front of it, and coming back a logic of observers and pistons runs the single-cell moves it takes to pull every piston and the block home, one at a time. mctas circuit pext anneal --flip-soft (each failed flip test of the real-use audit, at all 4 rotations, costs more than any size) reworked that logic, adding a slime block, until every early flip ends extended or at rest (pext).
