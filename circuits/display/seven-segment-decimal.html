7-segment display: one digit, 0 to 9, from a container's fill level

shows 0⁠–⁠9 from a container's fill level · 496 blocks · reads at a glance · one digit

One comparator reads a chest (or any container) beside it, and its 3×5 lamp face shows the signal, 0 to 9, as a digit: the fuller the container, the higher the digit. A new digit shows within 1.6 s, with no wrong lamp flickering on between digits.

passed at every position and facing we tested · no wrong lamp flashes between digits · fits in 13×10×22

After building it, put a chest (or any container) where the view shows the chest tagged A, and a redstone lamp in each of the 13 cells of its face: the download has none of them (our test adds them, and they do not count in its blocks). One digit only: the build is 13 blocks wide (the face is 3 of them), so copies stand 13 blocks apart and cannot make a two-digit number yet. It is tested for signals 0 to 9; a fuller container (signal 10 to 15) shows no digit we checked.

Try it

Press play under the view to watch our test on a loop: it fills the chest tagged A to every level from 0 to 9, one change at a time, and the face shows each digit. The A button under the view only empties the chest or fills it to the top (15, beyond the digits it shows).

How this design does it: One digit 0-9 from any container's fill level, read by one comparator: three stacked dust ladders turn the level into thresholds, and each segment ORs its runs (torches, taps and subtract comparators) on a dust bus into the face.

Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

one comparator reads a chest (or any container), and its 3×5 lamp face shows the signal as a digit, 0 to 9: the fuller the container, the higher the digit

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch our test on a loop: it fills the chest tagged A to every level from 0 to 9, one change at a time (every change between two digits), and the face shows each digit; the badge names the step. The A button under the view only empties the chest or fills it to the top (15, beyond the digits it shows). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom.

Our test in the real game

The judge drives the input with this schedule and checks the output after every change; here it is simulated (the 3D view's demo plays the same). Lime: an input is on; orange: the output is on (taller = a stronger signal, 1 to 15). Time runs left to right in game ticks (20 gt = 1 s).

Swipe the diagram sideways: the whole test lasts 3461 ticks (2 min 53.05 s).

Measurements

Measured in our test in the real game, facing all 4 ways at 4 spots in the world. gt = game tick = 1/20 s (20 gt = 1 second).

metricvaluewhat it means
latency32 gt (1.6 s; the slowest change: 0 to 2)the longest from the container's level changing to the whole face showing the new digit, over every change between two digits (the slowest change is named)
glitches25lamp changes that come and go within a change between two digits, too brief to see (under 4 game ticks); counted, not shown
on-blips0a lamp that lights wrongly for long enough to see (4 game ticks or more) during a change between two digits: 0 means no wrong lamp ever flashes

For comparison: a straightforward one-hot decoder built by our generator takes 1,668 blocks for the same digit.

Notes from its designer

One digit 0-9 from any container's fill level, read by one comparator: three stacked dust ladders turn the level into thresholds, and each segment ORs its runs (torches, taps and subtract comparators) on a dust bus into the face. 496 blocks, 1.6 s, no wrong lamp flashes between values; two digits side by side need pitch 13. The obvious decoder (one-hot plus a 49-tap ROM) is 1668 blocks.
