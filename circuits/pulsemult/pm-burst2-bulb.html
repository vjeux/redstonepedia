Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

Each input pulse gives exactly 2 output pulses (2 gt each, one every 6 gt): a pulse multiplier. Builds use it to fire a dispenser or a dropper exactly 2 times, or push a piston 2 times, from one button press.

The first comes 12 gt (0.6 s) after the input; the whole burst takes 20 gt (1 s).

Any input of 2 gt (the shortest a real input makes) or longer gives the same burst (a button, a plate or a held lever alike). A second press during the burst: one that comes within a few game ticks is swallowed, a later one adds a second burst. Pressed again and again it keeps up with presses 12 gt (0.6 s) apart: fed a clock that fast, it multiplies its frequency by 2.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop: single input pulses of 1 to 40 gt (each must give exactly 2 output pulses), then pairs of presses closer and closer together (what a second press does), then trains of 6 presses faster and faster (how fast it keeps up). The badge reads demo with the step it is on, and the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below. Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse: each tap gives exactly 2 flashes of the output.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

One press gives exactly two pulses: a copper bulb notices the press, and a short delay plus a second observer turn that one event into two blips. Detail: the bulb toggles on each rising edge, its comparator changes, an observer makes that a 2 tick pulse, a delay-3 repeater stretches it to 6 ticks, and a second observer pulses on both of the repeater's edges
