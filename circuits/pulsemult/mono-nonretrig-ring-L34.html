Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

Each input pulse, however short, turns the output on for 34 gt (1.7 s): a one-shot timer. It is non-retriggerable: input pulses that come while the output is on are ignored (one shot per press, however hard the button is mashed).

The output turns on 12 gt (0.6 s) after the input. It reacts to input pulses of 2 gt (the shortest a real input makes) or longer. Two input pulses at least 40 gt (2 s) apart give two full output pulses.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop: single input pulses of 1 to 160 gt (each gives one output pulse of 34 gt (1.7 s)), then pairs of short pulses closer and closer together: a second pulse while the output is on is ignored. The badge reads demo with the step it is on, and the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below. Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse: a tap keeps the output on for 34 gt (1.7 s); tapping again while it is on changes nothing.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

Gives one 34-tick output pulse per input pulse and ignores new inputs until it has finished: the input refills a small fading loop through a gate that the loop keeps shut while it runs. Unlike the hopper one-shot of the pulse study (8-tick steps, items to bring back before the next press), it times in 2-tick steps and is ready again a few ticks after its pulse. Details: a copper bulb, a comparator, an observer and a delay-3 repeater turn each rising edge into a 6-tick charge through a subtract comparator; the loop's own signal feeds that comparator's side, so edges during the pulse pass nothing; 3 dust return the signal, losing 2 per 4-tick lap.
