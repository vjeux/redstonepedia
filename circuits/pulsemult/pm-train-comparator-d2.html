Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

While the input is on, the output pulses every 12 gt (1.67 pulses a second, each 6 gt long): a clock with an on switch, for rapid fire from dispensers and droppers while a lever is on.

The first pulse comes 2 gt (0.1 s) after the input turns on. When the input turns off, it stops at once.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop: the input held on for 20, 60 and then 200 gt: the output pulses every 12 gt (0.6 s) while it is on and stops after. The badge reads demo with the step it is on, and the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below. Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: tap the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to turn that input on or off (it powers the cell with a redstone block): turn the input on and the output pulses every 12 gt (0.6 s) until you turn it off.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the orange glass marker next to it glows while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

While the input is held on it beats like a metronome, one short pulse every 12 ticks, and it stops as soon as the input goes off. Detail: a comparator in subtract mode whose own output comes back to its side through dust and a delay-2 repeater, so it switches itself off and on again; the first pulse comes right after the input turns on
