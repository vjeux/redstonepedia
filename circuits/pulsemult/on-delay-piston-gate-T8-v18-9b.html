Details: how it works, measurements and properties

In depth

The output turns on only once the input has been on for 8 ticks (0.4 s), and turns off with it: shorter inputs give nothing at all. It filters out glitches (the 1-2 ticks pulses of observers, flicker) and makes "hold the button" controls.

An input longer than 8 ticks (0.4 s) comes out shortened by 6 ticks (0.3 s); the output falls in the same game tick as the input.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the demo on a loop: input pulses of 1 to 80 ticks (4 s): the ones shorter than 8 ticks (0.4 s) give nothing, the longer ones come out shortened. The badge reads demo with the step it is on, and the same sequence is drawn as a timing diagram below. Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2-tick pulse: a quick tap gives nothing; hold it longer than 8 ticks (0.4 s) and the output lights until you let go.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the view adds it to show the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Inputs and output over time

The input follows this schedule (the 3D view's demo plays the same). Lime: an input is on; orange: the output is on (taller = a stronger signal, 1 to 15). Time runs left to right in game ticks (20 ticks = 1 s).

Swipe the diagram sideways: the whole sequence lasts 682 ticks (34.1 s).
