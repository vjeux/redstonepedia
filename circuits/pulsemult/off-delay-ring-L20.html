Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

The output turns on with the input (in the same game tick) and stays on 20 gt (1 s) after the input turns off: every output pulse is the input's length plus 20 gt (1 s). Input pulses closer together than that merge into one long output pulse (a light or a door that stays on a while after the last press of a pressure plate).

It reacts to input pulses of 4 gt (0.2 s) or longer.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop: input pulses of 1 to 80 gt (each output pulse lasts 20 gt (1 s) longer than its input), then groups of 3 short pulses with growing gaps (closer than L, they merge into one output pulse). The badge reads demo with the step it is on, and the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below. Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: tap the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to turn that input on or off (it powers the cell with a redstone block): turn the input on, then off: the output stays on 20 gt (1 s) after you turn it off.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

Keeps its output on for 20 ticks after the input turns off: two comparators pass the signal around a small loop, and the dust in the loop weakens it on every lap until it dies out. Unlike the 6-block item timer (off-delay-item-timer-L990), it restarts from a 2-tick input and times in 2-tick steps. Details: 5 dust carry the second comparator's output back to the first, losing 4 signal strength per 4-tick lap, and the output reads the loop where the signal lasts longest; while the input is on it keeps the loop full.
