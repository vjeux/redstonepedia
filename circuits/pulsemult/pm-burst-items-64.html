Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

Each input pulse gives exactly 64 output pulses (2 gt each, one every 4 gt): a pulse multiplier. Builds use it to fire a dispenser or a dropper exactly 64 times, or push a piston 64 times, from one button press.

The first comes 8 gt (0.4 s) after the input; the whole burst takes 262 gt (13.1 s).

Any input of 2 gt (the shortest a real input makes) or longer gives the same burst (a button, a plate or a held lever alike). A second press during the burst: breaks. Pressed again and again it keeps up with presses > 40 gt apart: fed a clock that fast, it multiplies its frequency by 64.

Notes from its designer

Gives 64 output pulses (2 ticks on, one every 4 ticks) for each input, and stays 25 blocks for any count: the count is the number of items in a dropper (61 items for 64 pulses, up to 579 pulses with 9 full stacks). Details: the input cell is a copper bulb; its comparator and an observer give one pulse per input rising edge, which toggles a second bulb on. The comparator reading that bulb locks the return hopper and powers a sticky piston that pushes an observer face to face with another, and the pair fires the dropper every 4 ticks, one item into the hopper each time; the other observer's back is the output. When the dropper is empty, its comparator turns off and the torch on its output block lights, toggling the bulb off: the piston pulls the observer away and the hopper returns the items, 8 ticks each.

# Draft (clk-rt, 2026-10-20): an item burst. Each input gives one output pulse per item in dropper

#   B1 off. P pulls B away and H returns the items to D.
