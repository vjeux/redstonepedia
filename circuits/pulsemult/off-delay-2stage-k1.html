Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

The output turns on with the input (6 gt (0.3 s) later) and stays on 108 gt (5.4 s) after the input turns off: every output pulse is the input's length plus 108 gt (5.4 s). Input pulses closer together than that merge into one long output pulse (a light or a door that stays on a while after the last press of a pressure plate).

It reacts to input pulses of 3 gt (0.15 s) or longer.

Notes from its designer

Keeps the output on for 108 ticks after the input turns off (a long off-delay): a small fast loop keeps a bigger, slower loop topped up, and once the input is gone the big loop counts down; each extra pair of comparators in the big loop adds 56 ticks. Details: stage 1 is a two-comparator loop (4-tick lap) that the input holds full; once the input stops it decays 2 per lap, so its output lasts at least 28 ticks. A repeater reading it powers stage 2, a three-comparator loop (6-tick lap) that loses only 1 per lap; stage 1's long output refills it evenly at full strength, and once stage 1 dies stage 2 counts its 15 levels down and the output goes dark at 0. Why not an item timer (a hopper draining k items at 8 ticks each, about 6 blocks for any length): the items move back only one per 8 ticks too, so an item timer needs an input about as long as its delay before it can time again, and it only comes in 8-tick steps; this loop refills in 3 ticks of input and keeps 2-tick resolution. For delays of many seconds an item timer is the smaller build.
