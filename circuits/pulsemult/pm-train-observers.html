Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

While the input is on, the output pulses every 4 gt (5 pulses a second, each 2 gt long): a clock with an on switch, for rapid fire from dispensers and droppers while a lever is on.

The first pulse comes 2 gt (0.1 s) after the input turns on. When the input turns off, no pulse starts more than 4 gt (0.2 s) later.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop: the input held on for 20, 60 and then 200 gt: the output pulses every 4 gt (0.2 s) while it is on and stops after. The badge reads demo with the step it is on, and the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below. Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: tap the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to turn that input on or off (it powers the cell with a redstone block): turn the input on and the output pulses every 4 gt (0.2 s) until you turn it off.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Measured in our test in the real game, facing all 4 ways at 4 spots in the world. gt = game tick = 1/20 s (20 gt = 1 second).

latency2 gt (0.1 s)game ticks from the input turning on to the first output pulse

Notes from its designer

While the input is held on, two observers stare at each other and keep firing, a pulse every 4 ticks; it stops when the input goes off. Detail: 3 blocks: the input extends a sticky piston that pushes one observer face to face with the other (the chase), and retracting it breaks the pair apart
