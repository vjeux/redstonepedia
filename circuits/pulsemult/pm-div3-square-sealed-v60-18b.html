Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

It divides a clock by 3: for every 3 pulses that come in, the output goes through one on-off cycle, so it runs 3 times slower than its input. Its output is a square wave: on for half of each cycle, off for the other half (for an odd N like 3 that takes both edges of the input clock: the output changes every 1.5 input pulses).

Each output cycle starts 39 gt (1.95 s) after the input pulse that begins it, always the same. It keeps up with input clocks down to one pulse every 20 gt (1 pulse a second); faster ones break the count.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop: a square clock on the input, first slow (a pulse every 40 gt, 2 s), then faster and faster down to a pulse every 2 gt; the output lamp goes through one on-off cycle for every 3 input pulses, until the clock gets faster than the design can follow. The badge reads demo with the step it is on, and the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below. Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse: tap it 3 times and the output goes through one on-off cycle.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

Divide-by-3 square wave, dropper ring, sealed: turns a fast clock into one 3 times slower with an even on/off split. pm-div3-square-sealed's mechanism (a ring of 3 droppers passing one item around, one step per input edge, and a copper bulb that flips each time the item comes back around; no hopper top a stray item can reach) rearranged by clk-rt's circuit compact under the item-intrusion judge: judged box 60 (was 90), 18 blocks (was 20); its blocks sit in 2x6x5.
