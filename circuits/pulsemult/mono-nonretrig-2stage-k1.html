Try it

Hold IN under the view for a long press, tap it for a short one: a tap keeps OUT red for a while; tapping again while it is red changes nothing (the box marked OUT turns red while the output is on). Press play under the view to watch our test on a loop; touch any other control to take over.

How this design does it: Gives one 118-tick output pulse per input pulse and ignores new inputs until it has finished (a long one-shot): the input fills a small fast loop through a gate, the small loop keeps a bigger slower loop topped up, and the big loop counts down while holding the gate shut; each extra pair of comparators in the big loop adds 56 ticks.

Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

Each input pulse, however short, turns the output on for 118 gt (5.9 s): a one-shot timer. It is non-retriggerable: input pulses that come while the output is on are ignored (one shot per press, however hard the button is mashed).

The output turns on 20 gt (1 s) after the input. It reacts to input pulses of 2 gt (the shortest a real input makes) or longer. Two input pulses at least 160 gt (8 s) apart give two full output pulses.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop: single input pulses of 1 to 160 gt (each gives one output pulse of 118 gt (5.9 s)), then pairs of short pulses closer and closer together: a second pulse while the output is on is ignored. The badge reads demo with the step it is on, and the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below. Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse: a tap keeps the output on for 118 gt (5.9 s); tapping again while it is on changes nothing.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Notes from its designer

Gives one 118-tick output pulse per input pulse and ignores new inputs until it has finished (a long one-shot): the input fills a small fast loop through a gate, the small loop keeps a bigger slower loop topped up, and the big loop counts down while holding the gate shut; each extra pair of comparators in the big loop adds 56 ticks. Details: a copper bulb, a comparator, an observer and a delay-3 repeater charge stage 1 through a subtract comparator whose side is fed from between the two loops, so every edge is ignored until the output itself has ended; stage 2 is a three-comparator loop (6-tick lap) that loses only 1 per lap. Why not an item timer (a hopper draining k items at 8 ticks each): it would need the input held about as long as the pulse to load its items, where this one fires on a 2-tick input. For one-shots of many seconds an item timer is the smaller build.

# Two comparator rings (crates/circuit-timer/src/long.rs). Stage 1 (two comparators, round 4 gt): a rising edge charges stage 1 through a gate (copper bulb, comparator K, observer O1, a delay-3 repeater, a subtract comparator into I1 whose side a repeater feeds from the dust between stage 1's stone A1 and stage 2's stone A2: every edge is ignored until the output itself has ended); stage 2 sits north of stage 1, fed through a repeater, a dust line and a second repeater; once its charge stops it decays 2 per round, so its output lasts at least 28 gt. A repeater reading its stone A1 powers stage 2's stone I2: stage 2 is a ring of 3 comparators (round 6 gt) that loses 1 per round, refilled evenly at 15 by stage 1's long output; once stage 1 dies it counts its levels down, and the output lamp beside its stone A2 goes dark at 0.
