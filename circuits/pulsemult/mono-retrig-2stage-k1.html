Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

Each input pulse, however short, turns the output on for 116 gt (5.8 s): a one-shot timer. It is retriggerable: another input pulse while the output is on restarts the count, so the output stays on while pulses keep coming and turns off 116 gt (5.8 s) after the last one (a light that stays on while there is activity; inverted, an alarm for a clock that stopped).

The output turns on 10 gt (0.5 s) after the input. It reacts to input pulses of 2 gt (the shortest a real input makes) or longer. Two presses at least 120 gt (6 s) apart give two separate output pulses; closer together, the output just stays on longer.

How to try it

Notes from its designer

Gives a 116-tick output pulse after each input pulse, and a new input restarts it (a long retriggerable one-shot): each input fills a small fast loop that keeps a bigger, slower loop topped up, which then counts down; each extra pair of comparators in the big loop adds 56 ticks. Details: a copper bulb, a comparator and two observers turn each rising edge into 4 ticks of full-strength signal for stage 1, a two-comparator loop (4-tick lap) that decays 2 per lap, so its output lasts at least 28 ticks; a repeater reading it refills stage 2, a three-comparator loop (6-tick lap) that loses only 1 per lap, which then counts its 15 levels down. Why not an item timer (a hopper draining k items at 8 ticks each): refilling it takes about as long as the pulse itself, so a new input could not restart it at once; this one restarts on any 2-tick input. For one-shots of many seconds an item timer is the smaller build.

# Two comparator rings (crates/circuit-timer/src/long.rs). Stage 1 (two comparators, round 4 gt): each rising edge charges stage 1 with 4 gt of 15 (copper bulb, comparator K, observer O1 down into a stone and a cross dust, observer O2 watching O1 into the dust on top of I1); once its charge stops it decays 2 per round, so its output lasts at least 28 gt. A repeater reading its stone A1 powers stage 2's stone I2: stage 2 is a ring of 3 comparators (round 6 gt) that loses 1 per round, refilled evenly at 15 by stage 1's long output; once stage 1 dies it counts its levels down, and the output lamp beside its stone A2 goes dark at 0.
