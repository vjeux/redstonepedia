Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

Each input pulse, however short, turns the output on for 32 gt (1.6 s): a one-shot timer. It is retriggerable: another input pulse while the output is on restarts the count, so the output stays on while pulses keep coming and turns off 32 gt (1.6 s) after the last one (a light that stays on while there is activity; inverted, an alarm for a clock that stopped).

The output turns on 4 gt (0.2 s) after the input. It reacts to input pulses of 2 gt (the shortest a real input makes) or longer. Two presses at least 40 gt (2 s) apart give two separate output pulses; closer together, the output just stays on longer.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop: single input pulses of 1 to 160 gt (each gives one output pulse of 32 gt (1.6 s)), then pairs of short pulses closer and closer together: a second pulse while the output is on restarts the count, so the output turns off L after the second one. The badge reads demo with the step it is on, and the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below. Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse: a tap keeps the output on for 32 gt (1.6 s); tap again while it is on and it stays on 32 gt (1.6 s) after your last tap.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

Gives a 32-tick output pulse after each input pulse, and a new input restarts the 32 ticks: every input refills a small two-comparator loop that fades out on its own. Unlike an item timer, a 2-tick press gives the whole length, in 2-tick steps, with no items to load back. Details: a copper bulb, a comparator and two observers turn each rising edge into 4 ticks of full-strength signal for the loop; 3 dust carry the signal back around, losing 2 per 4-tick lap, the least a two-comparator loop can lose, which is why this long pulse is also one of the smallest.
