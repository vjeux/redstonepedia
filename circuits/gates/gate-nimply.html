Try it

Use the A and B buttons under the view to drive the inputs: the box marked OUT turns red while the output is on. Press play under the view to watch our test on a loop; touch any other control to take over.

How this design does it: NIMPLY gate (A and not B), 3 blocks: a subtract comparator reads input A's block behind it, and a dust lying ON input B's block sits at its side; the comparator passes A minus B, so the output lights only while A is on and B is off.

In depth

A logic gate: the output depends only on the inputs right now. NIMPLY: the output is on only while A is on and B is off (A AND NOT B).

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: tap the A button of the bar (or the cell tagged A) to turn that input on or off (it powers the cell with a redstone block); tap the B button of the bar (or the cell tagged B) to turn that input on or off (it powers the cell with a redstone block).

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Our test in the real game

The judge drives the inputs with this schedule and checks the output after every change; here it is simulated (the 3D view's demo plays the same). Lime: an input is on; orange: the output is on (taller = a stronger signal, 1 to 15). Time runs left to right in game ticks (20 gt = 1 s).

Swipe the diagram sideways: the whole test lasts 281 ticks (14.05 s).
