Binary counter: shows how many times it was pressed, in binary

counts presses from 0 to 15 in binary · 15 blocks

Each press of its button adds one to the count, shown in binary on its 4 outputs (Q0 counts 1, Q1 counts 2, Q2 counts 4 and Q3 counts 8): it counts from 0 to 15, then starts again at 0.

smallest for its size of 2 tested · 3.8 blocks per bit · reacts in 14 ticks (0.7 s) · made of comparator, copper bulb, redstone torch · fits in 4×5×3

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: click the button tagged CLK (or the CLK button of the bar) to press it.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged Q0, Q1, Q2, Q3: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

A ripple counter of copper bulbs wound up a spiral stair. A copper bulb flips (lit / unlit) each time its power rises; the button on bulb 0 flips it at every press. A comparator reads each bulb (15 while it is lit) into a stone, which powers that bit's output; a torch on the side of that stone is lit while the bulb is unlit and powers the next bulb, which stands on the torch: when a bulb goes out, its torch lights and flips the next bulb, so each bit flips when the bit below it goes from 1 to 0 (counting up). Each comparator stands on the stone of the stage below; only the first needs a floor.
