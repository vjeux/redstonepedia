Details: how it works, measurements and properties

In depth

The output is a copy of the input, N = 7 ticks (0.35 s) later: a pulse goes in, the same pulse comes out N later, same length.

Pulses shorter than 6 ticks (0.3 s) get lost. Two pulses closer than 5 ticks (0.25 s) merge.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the demo on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same sequence is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2-tick pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the view adds it to show the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

N7 ticks (0.35 s)the delay: how much later the output copies the input
min-pulse6 ticks (0.3 s)the shortest input pulse handled correctly (every longer one works too)
min-gap5 ticks (0.25 s)the shortest gap between two input pulses that still comes out as two pulses

Notes from its designer

Delay line, 7 ticks, 10 blocks: the odd delays a repeater line cannot make. Two stages of two sticky pistons with redstone blocks, joined by a repeater set to 1. In each stage the first piston pushes its block against the second piston, which pulls its own block away from the next part. A piston set off by a block ARRIVING acts one tick later than one set off by a signal, so each stage takes 3 ticks one way and 2 the other, and inverts; two stages in a row add up to the same odd number on both edges.
