Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

The output is a copy of the input, N = 5 gt (0.25 s) later: a pulse goes in, the same pulse comes out N later, same length.

Pulses shorter than 6 gt (0.3 s) get lost. Two pulses closer than 5 gt (0.25 s) merge.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Measurements

Measured in our test in the real game, facing all 4 ways at 4 spots in the world. gt = game tick = 1/20 s (20 gt = 1 second).

metricvaluewhat it means
N5 gt (0.25 s)the delay: how much later the output copies the input
min-pulse6 gt (0.3 s)the shortest input pulse handled correctly (every longer one works too)
min-gap5 gt (0.25 s)the shortest gap between two input pulses that still comes out as two pulses

Notes from its designer

A 5-tick delay in 11 blocks: the signal comes out exactly 5 ticks later on both edges, which a repeater line (even ticks only) cannot do. The input pushes a redstone block (top left) next to the middle piston and, through a repeater set to 1, makes the piston behind it push a second block next to the same piston from the other side 2 ticks later; the middle piston fires 1 tick after the first block lands (a piston always waits a tick after a block arrives), and the second block keeps it out until 2 ticks after the input falls. It pushes away the redstone block that holds the output piston (top right) extended, so that piston pulls its own block next to the output 5 ticks after the rise; when the middle piston pulls the block back, the output piston waits its tick again and pushes its block away 5 ticks after the fall.
