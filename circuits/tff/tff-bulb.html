Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

Each input pulse flips the output: the first pulse turns it on, the next turns it off, and so on; between pulses the output stays put. It flips when the input turns on, 2 gt (0.1 s) later.

It works like a push-button lamp switch: press once for on, press again for off. It accepts input pulses of 1 gt (0.05 s) or longer and follows pulses as fast as one every 2 gt (0.1 s).

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the IN button of the bar (or the cell tagged IN) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

T flip-flop, 3 blocks: the input powers a waxed copper bulb, which toggles on every rising edge of its power; a comparator on a stone reads the bulb and is the output. Latency 2 ticks, and it follows pulses every 4 ticks. For a toggle on the FALLING edge, hang the 1-block NOT (a wall torch) on the input block and let the torch power the bulb. A row of them tiles at pitch 2: at pitch 1 the comparators would sit side by side and each one's side input would suppress its neighbour (ComparatorBlock.getAlternateSignal).
