Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

It keeps lighting TNT forever without using up its TNT block: about 22 TNT a minute. Every 54 gt (2.7 s) a piston pushes the TNT block: a piston copies every block it moves before it clears the old cells, and a TNT that gets a block update while powered at that instant is lit where it stands (a primed TNT that falls and explodes 80 gt, 4 s, later) while its moved copy lands as a TNT block again. Each push lights 1 TNT.

How to try it

The duper runs by itself as soon as it is built: the view plays it from the start. Watch the piston push the TNT block every cycle and a lit TNT (flashing white) drop out of the machine each time, while the TNT block lands again in its place; a lit TNT vanishes when its fuse runs out instead of exploding. Pause and step one game tick at a time with the bar's buttons; drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom.

Notes from its designer

Precise air burst, 44 blocks in a 5x5x10 box (250), 22.2 TNT/min: every lit TNT explodes at exactly the same point in the air, bit for bit, safe on the ground. clk-rt's minimum-volume search: the 45-block 250-volume version with one block fewer. The 16b engine is held by a redstone torch that a 7-block catalog P54 clock darkens for one TNT per period; the TNT flies into fence posts over a water channel, the current pins it against a fence post standing on a slime block, and the next TNT's push, carried by observers, makes the sticky piston under the slime launch it straight up, so it explodes near the top of its flight, more than 7 blocks from anything a blast could break. material: 2 obsidian -> stone (the same kind, axis and metrics on its judge); obsidian at 5 1 -2 stays for immovability (pistons must not move it: the design fails or changes even with a blast-proof netherite block there); obsidian at 6 0 -7 stays for immovability (pistons must not move it: the design fails or changes even with a blast-proof netherite block there)
