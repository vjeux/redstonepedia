Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

Each input pulse lights 1 TNT (33 gt (1.65 s) after the pulse) and the TNT block is back in place for the next pulse: the TNT is never used up. Pulses must be at least 100 gt (5 s) apart.

How: a piston copies every block it moves before it clears the old cells, and a TNT that gets a block update while powered at that instant is lit where it stands while its moved copy lands as a TNT block again.

Notes from its designer

One TNT per BUTTON press. The engine of tnt-triggered-launch-16b with a one-shot hold instead: the obsidian beside the piston is strongly powered by a wall torch under it, which holds the piston; the torch hangs on the stone under the piston (in the obsidian foot's place) and the button is on that stone. A press powers the stone: the stone holds the piston itself and the torch goes dark. At the release the stone goes quiet 2 ticks before the torch relights: the piston drops, the frame comes back, its redstone block repowers the piston through the dust and the push lights ONE TNT, however long the press. Latency: the press, then 5 ticks.
