TNT duper (runs by itself)

drops each TNT straight down to explode at one point · 30 TNT a minute · 54 blocks

Lights 30 TNT a minute while its lever is on, and every TNT falls straight down and explodes at the same point under the machine, without ever using up its own TNT block.

the smallest of 2 tested (counting the air it needs under it) that has an on/off lever · a push every 40 ticks (2 s) · fits in 8×5×8 + 14 blocks of air under it

Every TNT explodes 13.5 blocks under the machine, so stand it at least 14 blocks up for the blast to be in mid-air (our test stood it 16 blocks up); the machine itself survives from 5 blocks up. Needs water where the view shows it.

Try it

Flip its lever (the IN button under the view) to start it: watch a lit TNT come out of the machine each cycle while its TNT block stays in place; flip it back and it stops. The view first plays our test on a loop; touch any control to take over.

How this design does it: Precise drop with an on/off lever, ONE clock + ONE item-free delay: tnt-precise-drop (41b) with the 52b's lever inverter holding its clock, and its release chain made long enough that each TNT is released by its OWN push: the observer watching the engine's frame starts a line of six delay-4 repeaters (48 gt) to the repeater the trapdoor's observer watches.

Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

It keeps lighting TNT forever without using up its TNT block: 30 TNT a minute. Every 40 gt (2 s) a piston pushes the TNT block: a piston copies every block it moves before it clears the old cells, and a TNT that gets a block update while powered at that instant is lit where it stands (a primed TNT that falls and explodes 80 gt, 4 s, later) while its moved copy lands as a TNT block again. Each push lights 1 TNT.

How to try it

The duper runs by itself as soon as it is built: the view plays it from the start. Watch the piston push the TNT block every cycle and a lit TNT (flashing white) drop out of the machine each time, while the TNT block lands again in its place; a lit TNT vanishes when its fuse runs out instead of exploding. Pause and step one game tick at a time with the bar's buttons; drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom.

Our test in the real game
