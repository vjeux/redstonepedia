Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

It keeps lighting TNT forever without using up its TNT block: 200 TNT a minute. Every 6 gt (0.3 s) a piston pushes the TNT block: a piston copies every block it moves before it clears the old cells, and a TNT that gets a block update while powered at that instant is lit where it stands (a primed TNT that falls and explodes 80 gt, 4 s, later) while its moved copy lands as a TNT block again. Each push lights 1 TNT.

How to try it

The duper runs by itself as soon as it is built: the view plays it from the start. Watch the piston push the TNT block every cycle and a lit TNT (flashing white) drop out of the machine each time, while the TNT block lands again in its place; a lit TNT vanishes when its fuse runs out instead of exploding. Pause and step one game tick at a time with the bar's buttons; drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom.

Notes from its designer

The fast switchable TNT engine with the smallest EFFECTIVE volume: tnt-engine-lever-17b's engine (200 TNT/min) with its lever inverter UNDER the engine (the torch under the sticky piston on a stone, the lever on that stone's side) and a floor of 12 stone under the whole footprint. Why the floor: the safe drop of the floorless engines (6 for the 17b, 7 for the 3x3x5) is set by launched TNT that land about 20 blocks out and get knocked back by the explosions in their landing zone, exploding on the ground under the machine (per-spawn runs 58 and 51); the floor takes those blasts' rays. 3x5x4 box: 12 x (5 + 4) = 108, vs 150 (3x3x5) and 160 (the 17b).
