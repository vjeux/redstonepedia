Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

A one-bit memory. A pulse on S (set) turns the output on, and it stays on; a pulse on R (reset) turns it off, and it stays off. It remembers which one came last.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the R button of the bar (or the cell tagged R) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse; press and hold the S button of the bar (or the cell tagged S) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

Two droppers face each other and hold one stone between them. s (next to the dropper by S) fires that dropper and the stone goes to the other one; r fires the dropper by R and it comes back; firing the empty one does nothing, so repeated s or r change nothing. A comparator on stone reads the dropper by R: q is its output (1). Latency 6 gt (dropper 4 + comparator 2). A stack of them tiles with NO gap (`tile: 0 2 0`): a column of latches costs 4 blocks per bit.
