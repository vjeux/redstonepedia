Try it

Use the R and T buttons under the view to drive the inputs: each tap flips OUT on or off (the box marked OUT turns red while the output is on). Press play under the view to watch our test on a loop; touch any other control to take over.

How this design does it: T flip-flop with reset, dropper ring, sealed: tffr-ring-sealed's mechanism (two droppers and two hoppers pass one stone around; a comparator reading the second dropper is the output; every hopper top covered so no stray item reaches its timing) rearranged by clk-rt's circuit compact under the item-intrusion judge: judged box 18 (unchanged), 8 blocks (was 11); its blocks sit in 3x3x2.

In depth

a toggle switch with a reset input: each press flips the output, a press on R turns it off

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the R button of the bar (or the cell tagged R) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse; press and hold the T button of the bar (or the cell tagged T) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the orange glass marker next to it glows while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Our test in the real game

The judge drives the inputs with this schedule and checks the output after every change; here it is simulated (the 3D view's demo plays the same). Lime: an input is on; orange: the output is on (taller = a stronger signal, 1 to 15). Time runs left to right in game ticks (20 gt = 1 s).

Swipe the diagram sideways: the whole test lasts 1162 ticks (58.1 s).
