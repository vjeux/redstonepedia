Use the CLK, D and E buttons under the view to drive the inputs: the box marked OUT turns red while the output is on. Press play under the view to watch our test on a loop; touch any other control to take over.

How this design does it: D flip-flop with enable, 12 blocks: the 9-block flip-flop (two repeaters with lock repeaters beside them; the master's lock reads the clock block, a wall torch on that block feeds the slave's) plus one more lock on the slave's other side, fed by a wall torch on the enable block.

Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

a D flip-flop with an enable input: on the clock's edge it takes the data input only while the enable input is powered, otherwise it keeps its value

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the CLK button of the bar (or the cell tagged CLK) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse; press and hold the D button of the bar (or the cell tagged D) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse; press and hold the E button of the bar (or the cell tagged E) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged OUT: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Notes from its designer

D flip-flop with enable, 12 blocks: the 9-block flip-flop (two repeaters with lock repeaters beside them; the master's lock reads the clock block, a wall torch on that block feeds the slave's) plus one more lock on the slave's other side, fed by a wall torch on the enable block. A repeater is locked if EITHER side is powered, so while e is off the slave stays locked through any clock edge and q holds; no AND gate is needed.
