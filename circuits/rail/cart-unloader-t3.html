Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

A hopper minecart holds 320 items (5 stacks). A hopper under the cart pulls one item every 8 game ticks, 2.5 items a second, so an unloader is as fast as the hoppers that reach the cart: one under the cart empties a full one in about 2 minutes; two, by holding the cart where it straddles two blocks, in about 1. The station sends the cart back only once its hoppers find nothing more in it.

Notes from its designer

A hopper-minecart unloader at the end of a line: the line comes in one block up and the cart rolls down a detector-rail slope onto a powered rail over a hopper, stopping against the end block; the hopper empties it into the chest. The powered rail switches on only when the hopper is empty and the detector has been quiet for a moment, so a loaded cart waits until its last item has gone and is then pushed back up the line; an empty cart is sent back at once. Gravity on the slope brings even a crawling cart in quickly, so no booster is needed.

# CIRCUIT: cart-unloader-t3 cart-unloader line terminus, unload rate 2.48 items/s, departure delay 15 gt, 22 blocks, 5x3x3, robust
# cart-unloader terminus v3 (clk-obs, 2026-10-21): cart-unloader-t2 with the line coming in one
# block higher and the detector rail sloping down from it instead of a booster: gravity brings even
# a crawling cart down onto the parking rail inside the detector's hold (3 blocks fewer).
# The cart rolls down the detector-rail slope and over a plain rail onto a powered rail E over a
# hopper H, and stops against the end block. H pulls its items into the judge's chest below. E is
# powered (a torch inverter, a dust line, a repeater into the end block) only when no item is in H
# and the detector rail has been quiet for its 20 gt: an arriving cart parks, a loaded cart waits
# while H keeps getting items, an empty (or just emptied) cart is kicked away from the end block,
# back up the slope.
