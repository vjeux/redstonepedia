Half adder: adds two bits into a sum and a carry

half adder: two bits into a sum and a carry · 17 blocks

Adds two one-bit inputs, A and B: the sum output (S) is on when exactly one of them is on, the carry output (COUT) when both are.

passed at every position and facing we tested · reacts in 12 ticks (0.6 s) · fits in 4×2×3

Try it

Turn on A0, B0 or both with the buttons under the view: with one on, S0 (the sum) lights; with both, COUT (the carry) lights instead, because 1 + 1 = 10 in binary. Press play under the view to watch our test on a loop; touch any other control to take over.

How this design does it: A torch on input A and input B's wire meet in a comparator that is fully on when both are off, barely on when only B is on, and off when A is on.

How to try it

Press play under the view to watch the judge's own test on a loop (the badge reads demo with the step it is on; the same test is drawn as a timing diagram below). Touch any control to stop it and try the circuit yourself.

To try it: press and hold the A0 button of the bar (or the cell tagged A0) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse; press and hold the B0 button of the bar (or the cell tagged B0) to power that input; a quick tap sends a short 2 gt pulse.

Inputs are tagged in lime; the output is tagged COUT, S0: the redstone lamp in that cell (the lamp the judge itself adds to read the output) lights while the output is on (the output strip by the demo badge shows its last 40 game ticks, 2 seconds). Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, step one game tick at a time with the step button.

Our test in the real game

The judge drives the inputs with this schedule and checks the output after every change; here it is simulated (the 3D view's demo plays the same). Lime: an input is on; orange: the output is on (taller = a stronger signal, 1 to 15). Time runs left to right in game ticks (20 gt = 1 s).

Swipe the diagram sideways: the whole test lasts 521 ticks (26.05 s).

Notes from its designer

A torch on input A and input B's wire meet in a comparator that is fully on when both are off, barely on when only B is on, and off when A is on. The carry lights when B is on and that comparator is off (both inputs on); the sum lights when exactly one input is on. Found by a SAT search.
