Details: how it works in depth, our test in the real game, measurements and properties

In depth

A flying machine: pistons, slime or honey and observers that push each other along. Once started (the start block is placed), it moves itself one block at a time and keeps going until something stops it.

It moves one block every 10 gt (2.0 blocks per second).

How to try it

The machine starts by itself (its start block is placed when the view begins): press play and watch it shuttle between its docking stations. Flip its lever with the IN button under the view: off, it docks at the lever's station and stays; on, it leaves again. Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom.

Notes from its designer

Flying machine with a start lever, 7 blocks: a 6-block slime flying machine (a piston, a sticky piston, two slime blocks, two observers) parked next to a lever on a stone. Start: flip the lever, and the machine flies away at 10 ticks per block. The observer that watches the lever rides the part the first push moves, so flipping the lever again, at any moment, does nothing to the machine; the lever and its stone stay behind. found by `circuit compact` from track fly's `circuit fly start-scan --watcher`; the judge's start calibration is the same build.
