Clock

every 5 ticks (0.25 s) · 7 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 5 ticks (0.25 s), forever. Built in the game it needs one kick to start (the last block placed); then it keeps going.

A lamp on its output stays lit: the output is off for only 3 ticks (0.15 s) at a time, and a redstone lamp goes dark only after more than 4 ticks (0.2 s) without power. Redstone dust there follows every pulse. If its chunk unloads mid-cycle (you walk away and come back), it skips a beat, then keeps going.

made of observer, scaffolding, sticky piston · on for 2 of 5 ticks

Every clock that pulses every 5 ticks (0.25 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

The view starts it for you, then it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, take the signal from its observer (part of the download, where the view puts its lamp): it watches the clock and pulses every cycle.

How this design does it: A scaffolding clock: scaffolding S2 stands next to scaffolding S1 (on stone, distance 0); a sticky piston toggles a stone under S2 (2 gt observer pulses spit / pull it), so S2's distance and bottom flip between 0 (supported) and 1 (held by S1) one 1 gt scaffolding tick later; an observer watches S2, its back quasi-powering the piston through the air above it.

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 23 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 8 ticks (0.4 s); then it repeats every 5 ticks (0.25 s), on for 2 ticks (0.1 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
