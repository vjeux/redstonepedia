every 89 ticks (4.45 s) · 21 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 89 ticks (4.45 s), forever. It starts on its own once built.

Uses a sculk sensor: other sounds nearby (footsteps, another machine) can add pulses, so keep noise away from it.

A longer version of the tested clk-sculk-replin-dispenser clock, which pulses every 26 ticks (1.3 s) with 7 blocks; this one is 7 steps longer (n = 9). Each step: one more delay-4 repeater on a stone at the end of the line (the observer and dispenser move one block out): +8 gt of delay and +1 gt of travel, which adds 9 ticks (0.45 s) and 2 blocks. This length was simulated to exactly its period; the base design was tested in the real game.

Every clock that pulses every 89 ticks (4.45 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

It runs by itself: the view plays it; pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 353 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 86 ticks (4.3 s); then it repeats every 89 ticks (4.45 s), on for 10 ticks (0.5 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.

# The repeater line with an empty dispenser as the emitter: a calibrated sculk sensor, n delay-4 repeaters, an observer watching the last one and a dispenser behind it, whose failed dispense comes 4 gt after the observer's pulse (8n+6 gt after the sensor fires) and is heard n+2 gt later. Self-starting. P = 8 + 9n gt, 3 + 2n blocks (n = 9: P 89, 21 blocks). Built by `mctas clock sculk family replin-dispenser --n 9`.
name: clk-sculk-replin-dispenser-n9
