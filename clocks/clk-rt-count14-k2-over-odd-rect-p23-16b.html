Clock

every 322 ticks (16.1 s) · 42 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 322 ticks (16.1 s), forever. Built in the game it needs one kick to start (the last block placed); then it keeps going.

A lamp on its output stays lit: the output is off for only 2 ticks (0.1 s) at a time, and a redstone lamp goes dark only after more than 4 ticks (0.2 s) without power. Redstone dust there follows every pulse.

made of comparator, redstone torch, redstone wire, repeater · on for 320 of 322 ticks

Every clock that pulses every 322 ticks (16.1 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

Press play under the view to start it; then it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: Comparator COUNTER over another clock: a lossless comparator ring (2 + 2 comparators, one in subtract mode) loses 1 level(s) per pulse of the inner clock (its pulse cut to strength 1 by a subtract comparator with side 14) and its torch refills it to 14 at 0: period = 14 inner periods.

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 656 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 12 ticks (0.6 s); then it repeats every 322 ticks (16.1 s), on for 320 ticks (16 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.

Details: how it works, measurements and properties

Notes from its designer

Comparator COUNTER over another clock: a lossless comparator ring (2 + 2 comparators, one in subtract mode) loses 1 level(s) per pulse of the inner clock (its pulse cut to strength 1 by a subtract comparator with side 14) and its torch refills it to 14 at 0: period = 14 inner periods. Inner clock: ODD period 23: Rectangle ring (clk-rt): the 3x4 perimeter clockwise from the north-west corner = [b, 4*, b, 4, 4, b, 4, b, 4, 3] (number = repeater delay, * = powered at placement, b = block, d = dust, T = wall torch on the block before it, c = comparator); every redstone-tick delay is even).
