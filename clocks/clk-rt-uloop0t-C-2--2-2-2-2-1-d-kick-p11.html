Its output pulses by itself every 11 ticks (0.55 s), forever. Built in the game it needs one kick to start (the last block placed); then it keeps going.

made of redstone wire, repeater · on for 4 of 11 ticks

Every clock that pulses every 11 ticks (0.55 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

The view starts it for you, then it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: ODD period: U-loop: block A + dust beside it -> top row [delay-2 repeater (powered at placement), delay-2 repeater, delay-2 repeater] -> block F -> dust beside F -> bottom row [delay-2 repeater, delay-2 repeater, delay-1 repeater] -> A; 0 torches = a pulse ring of loop delay 22 gt; kicked by a redstone_block at [-1, 1, -1] for 1 gt at tick 7 (an odd tick: a second parity class; every redstone-tick delay is even)

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 48 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 15 ticks (0.75 s); then it repeats every 11 ticks (0.55 s), on for 4 ticks (0.2 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
