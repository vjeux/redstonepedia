Clock

every 31 ticks (1.55 s) · 4 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 31 ticks (1.55 s), forever. Built in the game it needs one kick to start (the last block placed); then it keeps going.

Uses a sculk sensor: other sounds nearby (footsteps, another machine) can add pulses, so keep noise away from it.

made of calibrated sculk sensor, dispenser, observer · on for 10 of 31 ticks

Every clock that pulses every 31 ticks (1.55 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

The view starts it for you, then it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: A calibrated sculk sensor watched by a chain of 2 observer(s) (each watching the previous); an empty dispenser (fires 4 gt after a rising edge) at the last output, 3 from the sensor (travel time): the observers' pulse after the sensor re-arms is heard

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 95 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 2 ticks (0.1 s); then it repeats every 31 ticks (1.55 s), on for 10 ticks (0.5 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
