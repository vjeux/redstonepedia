Clock

every 4 ticks (0.2 s) · 6 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 4 ticks (0.2 s), forever. Built in the game it needs one kick to start (the last block placed); then it keeps going.

A lamp on its output stays lit: the output is off for only 2 ticks (0.1 s) at a time, and a redstone lamp goes dark only after more than 4 ticks (0.2 s) without power. Redstone dust there follows every pulse. Stops for good if its chunk unloads mid-cycle (you walk away and come back): start it again the way you started it after building it.

made of observer, piston, sticky piston · 1 other block choice on its page · on for 2 of 4 ticks

Every clock that pulses every 4 ticks (0.2 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

The view starts it for you, then it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, take the signal from its observer (part of the download, where the view puts its lamp): it watches the clock and pulses every cycle.

How this design does it: P16: 1 copper bulb, 2 observers, 1 piston, 1 sticky piston; changing in place: observer, piston, sticky_piston (copper bulb only moved or static).

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 23 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 11 ticks (0.55 s); then it repeats every 4 ticks (0.2 s), on for 2 ticks (0.1 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
