Its output pulses by itself every 66 ticks (3.3 s), forever. It starts on its own once built.

Hard to wire into a circuit: we found no spot beside it where a repeater, a comparator or an observer picks up every pulse.

Comes loaded with 2 stone in its containers: the download includes them; building it by hand, put them in first.

A longer version of the tested dropper ring, sealed clock, which pulses every 38 ticks (1.9 s) with 23 blocks; this one is 2 steps longer (n = 2). Each step: make the dropper ring one column longer (2 more hoppers); sealed: glass on the open hopper tops (no dropped item reaches a hopper), which adds 14 ticks (0.7 s) and 4 blocks. Tested in the real game too.

Every clock that pulses every 66 ticks (3.3 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

It runs by itself: the view plays it; pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it.

How this design does it: The sealed twin of clk-item-dropring-1d-r2: each member is clk-item-dropring-1d-r2's with glass on its open hopper tops, so no dropped item can change its timing (clk-misc's audit: a dropped item changes or breaks the open family); period 38 + 14n, 23 + 4n blocks.

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 200 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 2 ticks (0.1 s); then it repeats every 66 ticks (3.3 s), on for 7 ticks (0.35 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
