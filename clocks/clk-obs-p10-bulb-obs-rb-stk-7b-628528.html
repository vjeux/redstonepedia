Clock

every 10 ticks (0.5 s) · 9 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 10 ticks (0.5 s), forever. Built in the game it needs one kick to start (the last block placed); then it keeps going.

A lamp on its output stays lit: the output is off for only 4 ticks (0.2 s) at a time, and a redstone lamp goes dark only after more than 4 ticks (0.2 s) without power. Redstone dust there follows every pulse. If its chunk unloads mid-cycle (you walk away and come back), it skips a beat, then keeps going.

made of copper bulb, observer, redstone block, sticky piston · on for 6 of 10 ticks

Try it

The view starts it for you, then it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, take the signal from its comparator (part of the download, where the view puts its lamp): it reads the clock and passes on every pulse.

How this design does it: P10: 1 copper bulb, 2 observers, 1 redstone block, 1 redstone lamp, 2 sticky pistons; changing in place: copper_bulb, observer, redstone_block, sticky_piston (redstone lamp only moved or static).

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 38 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 8 ticks (0.4 s); then it repeats every 10 ticks (0.5 s), on for 6 ticks (0.3 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
