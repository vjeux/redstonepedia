Try it

Press play under the view: it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: A clock that pulses exactly every 10 seconds, set by the 7 stones in its first hopper, and sealed: no dropped item can reach its hoppers.

The output (orange) over the first 604 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 4 ticks (0.2 s); then it repeats every 200 ticks (10 s), on for 100 ticks (5 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.

Details: how it works, measurements and properties

Notes from its designer

A clock that pulses exactly every 10 seconds, set by the 7 stones in its first hopper, and sealed: no dropped item can reach its hoppers. each hopper's comparator powers a sticky piston, and the two pistons hold one redstone block beside the hopper that receives (a locked hopper still receives) until the sender is empty; a stone covers each hopper. Copper bulbs (each toggled once per cycle and read by a comparator) double the time per bulb. Catalog parts: comparators, a repeater delay, copper-bulb T flip-flops as dividers.
