Clock

every 43 ticks (2.15 s) · 5 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 43 ticks (2.15 s), forever. Built in the game it needs one kick to start (the last block placed); then it keeps going.

Uses a sculk sensor: other sounds nearby (footsteps, another machine) can add pulses, so keep noise away from it. If its chunk unloads mid-cycle (you walk away and come back), it skips a beat, then keeps going.

made of observer, piston, sculk sensor · on for 30 of 43 ticks

Every clock that pulses every 43 ticks (2.15 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

The view starts it for you, then it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: A plain sculk sensor watched by a chain of 1 observer(s) (each watching the previous); the last observer powers a stone and a piston beside the stone, 2 from the sensor (travel time): the observers' pulse after the sensor re-arms is heard; plus a piston beside the chain (moves parts of it: the hybrid)

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 162 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 33 ticks (1.65 s); then it repeats every 43 ticks (2.15 s), on for 30 ticks (1.5 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
