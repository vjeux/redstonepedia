Clock

every 44 ticks (2.2 s) · 4 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 44 ticks (2.2 s), forever. It starts on its own once built.

Uses a sculk sensor: other sounds nearby (footsteps, another machine) can add pulses, so keep noise away from it. If its chunk unloads mid-cycle (you walk away and come back), it skips a beat, then keeps going.

made of observer, sculk sensor, sticky piston · on for 30 of 44 ticks

Every clock that pulses every 44 ticks (2.2 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

It runs by itself: the view plays it; pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: A plain sculk sensor watched by a chain of 1 observer(s) (each watching the previous); an oak trapdoor (placed open+powered: it closes on placement, the self-start) at the last output, 2 from the sensor (travel time): the observers' pulse after the sensor re-arms is heard; plus a sticky piston beside the chain (moves parts of it: the hybrid)

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 164 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 32 ticks (1.6 s); then it repeats every 44 ticks (2.2 s), on for 30 ticks (1.5 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
