A longer version of the 27-block clock that pulses every 72 ticks (3.6 s): 6 steps longer. Each step: append one master-slave T flip-flop stage (18 blocks: 2 latch repeaters, 2 lock repeaters, a target block with a wall torch, dust) to the chain on the 36-tick torch + repeater loop: the period doubles, which adds 756 ticks (37.8 s) and 18 blocks.

Every clock that pulses every 4,608 ticks (3 min 50.4 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

Press play under the view: it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: EXTENDABLE divider chain, n = 1 stages on a 1-torch U-loop: P = 36 x 2^n; each stage a master-slave T flip-flop (18 blocks)

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 4000 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 16 ticks (0.8 s); then it repeats every 4,608 ticks (3 min 50.4 s), on for 3,456 ticks (2 min 52.8 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
