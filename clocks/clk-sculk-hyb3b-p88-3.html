Clock

every 88 ticks (4.4 s) · 5 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 88 ticks (4.4 s), forever. It starts on its own once built.

Uses a sculk sensor: other sounds nearby (footsteps, another machine) can add pulses, so keep noise away from it. If its chunk unloads mid-cycle (you walk away and come back), it skips a beat, then keeps going. Hard to wire into a circuit: we found no spot beside it where a repeater, a comparator or an observer picks up every pulse.

made of iron trapdoor, observer, sculk sensor, sticky piston · also works with fence gate · on for 1 of 88 ticks

Every clock that pulses every 88 ticks (4.4 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

It runs by itself: the view plays it; pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it.

How this design does it: A plain sculk sensor watched by a chain of 1 observer(s) (each watching the previous); the last observer powers a stone and an iron trapdoor beside the stone, 3 from the sensor (travel time): the observers' pulse after the sensor re-arms is heard; plus a sticky piston beside the chain (moves parts of it: the hybrid)

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 297 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 33 ticks (1.65 s); then it repeats every 88 ticks (4.4 s), on for 1 tick (0.05 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
