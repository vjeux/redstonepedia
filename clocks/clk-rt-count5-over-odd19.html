Every clock that pulses every 95 ticks (4.75 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

Press play under the view to start it; then it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: Comparator COUNTER over another clock: a lossless comparator ring (2 + 2 comparators, one in subtract mode) loses 3 level(s) per pulse of the inner clock (its pulse cut to strength 3 by a subtract comparator with side 12) and its torch refills it to 14 at 0: period = 5 inner periods.

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 306 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 21 ticks (1.05 s); then it repeats every 95 ticks (4.75 s), on for 85 ticks (4.25 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.

Details: how it works, measurements and properties

Notes from its designer

Comparator COUNTER over another clock: a lossless comparator ring (2 + 2 comparators, one in subtract mode) loses 3 level(s) per pulse of the inner clock (its pulse cut to strength 3 by a subtract comparator with side 12) and its torch refills it to 14 at 0: period = 5 inner periods. Inner clock: ODD period 19: Rectangle ring (clk-rt): the 3x4 perimeter clockwise from the north-west corner = [b, 4*, b, 4, 4, b, 4, b, 3, d] (number = repeater delay, * = powered at placement, b = block, d = dust, T = wall torch on the block before it, c = comparator); every redstone-tick delay is even).
