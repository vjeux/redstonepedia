Clock

every 72 ticks (3.6 s) · 27 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 72 ticks (3.6 s), forever. It starts on its own once built. To make it slower: append one master-slave T flip-flop stage (18 blocks: 2 latch repeaters, 2 lock repeaters, a target block with a wall torch, dust) to the chain on the P36 torch + repeater loop: the period doubles; each step adds 72 ticks (3.6 s) and 18 blocks, up to every 4608 ticks (3 min 50.4 s).

Every clock that pulses every 72 ticks (3.6 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

It runs by itself: the view plays it; pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: EXTENDABLE divider chain, n = 1 stages on a 1-torch U-loop (delays 4/4, P36): P = 36 x 2^n; each stage a master-slave T flip-flop (18 blocks)

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 222 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 6 ticks (0.3 s); then it repeats every 72 ticks (3.6 s), on for 36 ticks (1.8 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
