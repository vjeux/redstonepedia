Clock

every 25 ticks (1.25 s) · 7 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 25 ticks (1.25 s), forever. Built in the game it needs one kick to start (the last block placed); then it keeps going.

Uses a sculk sensor: other sounds nearby (footsteps, another machine) can add pulses, so keep noise away from it. If its chunk unloads mid-cycle (you walk away and come back), it skips a beat, then keeps going.

made of calibrated sculk sensor, observer, sculk sensor, sticky piston · on for 10 of 25 ticks

Every clock that pulses every 25 ticks (1.25 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

The view starts it for you, then it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: A calibrated sculk sensor watched by a chain of 3 observer(s) (each watching the previous); the last observer powers a stone and a sticky piston beside the stone, 3 from the sensor (travel time): the observers' pulse after the sensor re-arms is heard; plus a sculk sensor beside the chain (moves parts of it: the hybrid)

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 82 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 7 ticks (0.35 s); then it repeats every 25 ticks (1.25 s), on for 10 ticks (0.5 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
