Clock

every 4 ticks (0.2 s) · 5 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 4 ticks (0.2 s), forever. Built in the game it needs one kick to start (the last block placed); then it keeps going.

A lamp on its output stays lit: the output is off for only 2 ticks (0.1 s) at a time, and a redstone lamp goes dark only after more than 4 ticks (0.2 s) without power. Redstone dust there follows every pulse.

made of note block, observer · 1 other block choice on its page · on for 2 of 4 ticks

Every clock that pulses every 4 ticks (0.2 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

The view starts it for you, then it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: P4: 1 note block, 2 observers, 1 piston, 1 redstone block; changing in place: note_block, observer (piston, redstone block only moved or static).

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 15 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 3 ticks (0.15 s); then it repeats every 4 ticks (0.2 s), on for 2 ticks (0.1 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.

Notes from its designer

P4: 1 note block, 2 observers, 1 piston, 1 redstone block; changing in place: note_block, observer (piston, redstone block only moved or static). Found by the clk-obs exhaustive census of every 5-block structure (all orientations, every block placed last as a kick); minimal: no single block can be removed keeping the period.
