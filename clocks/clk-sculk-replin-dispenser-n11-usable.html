Try it

It runs by itself: the view plays it; pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: A clock pulsing every 107 gt (5.35 s) with an output a builder can wire: clk-sculk's dispenser-fed sculk repeater line (member n = 11): a calibrated sculk sensor hears the dispenser, an observer and a repeater line carry the pulse back; a repeater on the line's end is the tap.

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 423 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 102 ticks (5.1 s); then it repeats every 107 ticks (5.35 s), on for 10 ticks (0.5 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.

# CLOCK: clk-sculk-replin-dispenser-n11-usable period 107 gt (5.35 s), on 10/107, edges 1, warmup 102, family calibrated_sculk_sensor+dispenser+observer+repeater, 25 blocks, 14x2x1, robust
# The repeater line with an empty dispenser as the emitter: a calibrated sculk sensor, n delay-4 repeaters, an observer watching the last one and a dispenser behind it, whose failed dispense comes 4 gt after the observer's pulse (8n+6 gt after the sensor fires) and is heard n+2 gt later. Self-starting. P = 8 + 9n gt, 3 + 2n blocks (n = 11: P 107, 25 blocks). Built by `mctas clock sculk family replin-dispenser --n 11`.
# usable tap (clk-obs `clock obs tap`): a repeater at [0, 1, -1] (on a block put under it), the output at [0, 1, -1]
