Clock

every 4 ticks (0.2 s) · 5 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 4 ticks (0.2 s), forever. Built in the game it needs one kick to start (the last block placed); then it keeps going.

A lamp on its output stays lit: the output is off for only 2 ticks (0.1 s) at a time, and a redstone lamp goes dark only after more than 4 ticks (0.2 s) without power. Redstone dust there follows every pulse. If its chunk unloads mid-cycle (you walk away and come back), it skips a beat, then keeps going.

made of note block, observer, sticky piston · also works with copper bulb, piston, redstone lamp · on for 2 of 4 ticks

Try it

The view starts it for you, then it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: P4: 1 note block, 3 observers, 1 sticky piston; changing in place: note_block, observer, sticky_piston.

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 23 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 11 ticks (0.55 s); then it repeats every 4 ticks (0.2 s), on for 2 ticks (0.1 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.

Notes from its designer

P4: 1 note block, 3 observers, 1 sticky piston; changing in place: note_block, observer, sticky_piston. Found by the clk-obs exhaustive census of every 5-block structure (all orientations, every block placed last as a kick); minimal: no single block can be removed keeping the period. Output: the changing observer with the most rising edges (declared, so every rotation reads the same cell).
