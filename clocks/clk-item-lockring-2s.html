every 33 ticks (1.65 s) · 11 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 33 ticks (1.65 s), forever. It starts on its own once built. To make it slower: one more tick of delay on the lock repeater (same blocks); each step adds 2 ticks (0.1 s), up to every 39 ticks (1.95 s).

Every clock that pulses every 33 ticks (1.65 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

It runs by itself: the view plays it; pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: 2x2 hopper ring with a delayed lock and 2 swords: the lock pair's wiring on a 4-hopper ring: the comparator reads the first ring hopper and powers a stone, the dust beside it feeds a repeater (delay n + 1) into the second ring hopper, locking it while the first holds a sword; the 2 swords start in the last ring hopper: P = 31 + 2 (n + 1) (37 and 39 included).

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 109 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 10 ticks (0.5 s); then it repeats every 33 ticks (1.65 s), on for 15 ticks (0.75 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
