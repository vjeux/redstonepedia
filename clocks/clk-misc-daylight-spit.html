Clock

every 40 ticks (2 s) · 5 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 40 ticks (2 s) while the sun is up. Built in the game it needs one kick to start (the last block placed); then it keeps going.

Uses a daylight detector: it needs open sky and runs only in daytime; it stops at night and in rain. Depends on where it is built: at another spot in the world its timing changed in our test, so try it where you build it. Stops for good if its chunk unloads mid-cycle (you walk away and come back) (in 1 of 8 of our test builds): start it again the way you started it after building it. Hard to wire into a circuit: we found no spot beside it where a repeater, a comparator or an observer picks up every pulse.

Notes from its designer

A daylight sensor sees the sky; a piston covers and uncovers it, so its reading flips every second while the sun is up. In detail: an inverted daylight detector reads 0 under the open sky and 1 when a block hides the sky right above it (sky light 14 from the sides). It updates only when the game time is a multiple of 20 gt. An observer on it pulses a sticky piston (through the stone under the piston) for 2 gt: a 2 gt pulse makes a sticky piston spit the block in front of it one cell further (here: over the detector) and the next pulse pull it back (the block 2 cells away is no longer moving). So every detector update toggles the cover and the next update (20 gt later) sees the change: P = 40 gt.
