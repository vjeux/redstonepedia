every 85 ticks (4.25 s) · 21 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 85 ticks (4.25 s), forever. It starts on its own once built.

Sounds near its sculk sensor (footsteps, another machine) add pulses: keep noise away from it.

A longer version of the 9-block clock that pulses every 31 ticks (1.55 s): 6 steps longer. Each step: one more delay-4 repeater on a stone at the end of the line (the observer and trapdoor move one block out): +8 ticks (0.4 s) of delay and +1 tick (0.05 s) of travel, which adds 9 ticks (0.45 s) and 2 blocks.

Every clock that pulses every 85 ticks (4.25 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

Press play under the view: it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 341 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 86 ticks (4.3 s); then it repeats every 85 ticks (4.25 s), on for 10 ticks (0.5 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.
