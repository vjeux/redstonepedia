Clock

every 10 ticks (0.5 s) · 8 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 10 ticks (0.5 s), forever. Built in the game it needs one kick to start (the last block placed); then it keeps going. To make it slower: insert 2 observers: the 2-wide ring one row higher; each step adds 4 ticks (0.2 s) and 2 blocks, up to every 198 ticks (9.9 s).

made of iron door, observer · on for 2 of 10 ticks

Every clock that pulses every 10 ticks (0.5 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

The view starts it for you, then it runs by itself: pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, take the signal from its observer (part of the download, where the view puts its lamp): it watches the clock and pulses every cycle.

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 34 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 4 ticks (0.2 s); then it repeats every 10 ticks (0.5 s), on for 2 ticks (0.1 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.

# CLOCK: clk-misc-iron_door-ring-n0 period 10 gt (0.5 s), on 2/10, edges 1, warmup 4, family iron_door+observer, 8 blocks, 2x4x2, robust
# Extendable family clk-misc-iron_door-ring (n = 0): P = 10 + 4n gt = 10 gt, 7 + 2n = 7 blocks. Relay: an iron door standing on the observer that powers its lower half: both halves open and close with that power at once.
# It sits at the top of a 2-wide ring of 5 observers (each watches the one before it, 2 gt per observer); the observer under it
# powers it and carries it, the next one watches it. The relay adds no delay but takes a lattice cell, so an odd number of
# observers closes the ring. The door's upper half is one more block. Extension: insert 2 observers (the ring one row higher).
# Generator: mctas clock misc ring --relay iron_door --n N --out FILE.
