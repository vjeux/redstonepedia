Clock

every 16 ticks (0.8 s) · 9 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 16 ticks (0.8 s), forever. It starts on its own once built.

Works only facing the way it is shown here: build it from the download without turning it; turned another way it stops or changes its timing (our test tried all 4 directions). If its chunk unloads mid-cycle (you walk away and come back), its period changes to 8 ticks (0.4 s) (in 3 of 8 of our test builds).

Try it

It runs by itself: the view plays it; pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: Digital P=16, 2x2 hopper ring with mixed items: 4 hoppers in a ring holding 2 swords and 2 stones (constructive census found it analog: the comparator reading one hopper sees a sword (3) or a stone (1), never 0).

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 69 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 21 ticks (1.05 s); then it repeats every 16 ticks (0.8 s), on for 7 ticks (0.35 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.

Notes from its designer

Digital P=16, 2x2 hopper ring with mixed items: 4 hoppers in a ring holding 2 swords and 2 stones (constructive census found it analog: the comparator reading one hopper sees a sword (3) or a stone (1), never 0). Here the reader is a SUBTRACT comparator whose side input is a constant 1 (a second comparator reading a dead-end hopper holding one stone): output max(s - 1, 0) = 2 or 0, digital, P = 16.
