every 67 ticks (3.35 s) · 17 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 67 ticks (3.35 s), forever. It starts on its own once built.

Uses a sculk sensor: other sounds nearby (footsteps, another machine) can add pulses, so keep noise away from it.

A longer version of the tested clk-sculk-replin (one block swapped: 2 versions) clock, which pulses every 31 ticks (1.55 s) with 9 blocks; this one is 4 steps longer (n = 7). Each step: one more delay-4 repeater on a stone at the end of the line (the observer and trapdoor move one block out): +8 gt of delay and +1 gt of travel, which adds 9 ticks (0.45 s) and 2 blocks. This length was simulated to exactly its period; the base design was tested in the real game.

Every clock that pulses every 67 ticks (3.35 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

It runs by itself: the view plays it; pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 271 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 70 ticks (3.5 s); then it repeats every 67 ticks (3.35 s), on for 10 ticks (0.5 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.

# The repeater line with an oak trapdoor as the emitter: a calibrated sculk sensor, n delay-4 repeaters, an observer watching the last one and a trapdoor behind it, opened on the observer's pulse 8n+2 gt after the sensor fires and heard n+2 gt later. Self-starting. P = 4 + 9n gt, 3 + 2n blocks (n = 7: P 67, 17 blocks). Built by `mctas clock sculk family replin-trapdoor --n 7`.
name: clk-sculk-replin-trapdoor-n7
