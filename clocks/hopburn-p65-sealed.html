Clock

every 65 ticks (3.25 s) · 10 blocks

Its output pulses by itself every 65 ticks (3.25 s), forever. It starts on its own once built.

A lamp on its output stays lit: the output is off for only 2 ticks (0.1 s) at a time, and a redstone lamp goes dark only after more than 4 ticks (0.2 s) without power. Redstone dust there follows every pulse.

Try it

It runs by itself: the view plays it; pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, put a repeater where the view puts its lamp: it picks up every pulse.

How this design does it: The 65 gt hopper burnout clock sealed: glass over its open hopper tops, so no dropped item can reach them (open, a dropped item made it an 8 gt clock); a repeater at the output takes the signal.

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 229 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 34 ticks (1.7 s); then it repeats every 65 ticks (3.25 s), on for 63 ticks (3.15 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.

# CLOCK: hopburn-p65-sealed period 65 gt (3.25 s), on 63/65, edges 1, warmup 34, family comparator+hopper+redstone_torch+redstone_wire, 10 blocks, 2x3x3, robust
# Burnout hybrid (found by clock item fill on the hopper pair): a wall torch on the stone next to the west-facing hopper, with a block above the torch and dust on the stone, burns out (8 flips, then the 60-gt window); while its dust is lit the stone locks that hopper, which shifts the 2-sword slosh and the comparator reading the other hopper by one move, so the comparator dips once per cycle and its update relights the torch: P = 65.
