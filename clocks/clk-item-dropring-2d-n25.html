A longer version of the tested dropper ring clock, which pulses every 34 ticks (1.7 s) with 14 blocks; this one is 25 steps longer (n = 25). Each step: make the dropper ring one column longer (2 more hoppers). This length was simulated to exactly its period; the base design was tested in the real game.

Every clock that pulses every 384 ticks (19.2 s): on the Clocks tab, smallest first (the ones hard to wire into a circuit last).

Try it

It runs by itself: the view plays it; pause and step one tick at a time with the buttons under it. To use it in a circuit, take the signal from its comparator (part of the download, where the view puts its lamp): it reads the clock and passes on every pulse.

How this design does it: Dropper ring: a hopper ring with dropper(s) in its bottom row; the comparator in the inner column reads the top-row hopper above it and fires the dropper below it (through the repeaters, each on stone).

Its output over time

The output (orange) over the first 770 ticks, as the 3D view plays it: the grey part is the clock starting up, for 2 ticks (0.1 s); then it repeats every 384 ticks (19.2 s), on for 7 ticks (0.35 s) of them. 20 ticks = 1 second.

Notes from its designer

Dropper ring: a hopper ring with dropper(s) in its bottom row; the comparator in the inner column reads the top-row hopper above it and fires the dropper below it (through the repeaters, each on stone). One stone waits in the dropper until the other passes the read hopper; then they swap roles. The ring runs counter-clockwise; P = 20 + 14 (W - 3) + 7 (depth - 3) + 7 [one dropper] + 2 x (sum of repeater delays): fourteen cards of this ring (one per P mod 14) reach every P from 42 up.
